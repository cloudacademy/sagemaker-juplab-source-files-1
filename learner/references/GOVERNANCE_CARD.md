# Governance reference card

Use NIST AI RMF 1.0 as a voluntary risk-management framework, not a certification or legal compliance checklist. Connect evidence to decisions and named owners.

- GOVERN: define approval authority, accountability, documentation, supplier expectations and review frequency.
- MAP: specify intended use, affected parties, harms, assumptions, operating context and limits.
- MEASURE: evaluate validity, reliability, resilience, explainability and relevant error disparities; document uncertainty and missing evidence.
- MANAGE: prioritise risks, select mitigations, define release gates, monitor operation and establish rollback or retirement triggers.

Reference: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework/nist-ai-rmf-playbook and https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/ (checked 7 October 2026).

For each residual risk, record the evidence, severity, mitigation, owner, remaining risk and what would allow acceptance. Controls proposed but not implemented and verified do not automatically reduce residual risk. Do not infer that a phishing classifier is an EU AI Act high-risk system merely because it is security related. If you introduce legal analysis, establish the jurisdiction and use-case applicability and use current official sources.
